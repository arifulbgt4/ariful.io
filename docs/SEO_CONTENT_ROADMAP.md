# SEO Content Roadmap

## Purpose and status

This is the canonical execution plan for qualified organic discovery of
ariful.io. It translates the portfolio's evidence-first positioning into a
small number of commercial pages, case studies, and substantial technical
articles.

It does not promise a Google number-one position. The near-term objective is
to earn relevant impressions, useful rankings, public-work review, and
qualified product conversations for a truthful, distinct entity:

> End-to-End Product Engineer for Software, AI & Connected/IoT Products.

Google's current guidance favors helpful, reliable, people-first content with
clear authorship, expertise, and original value over content made primarily to
manipulate rankings. A sitemap helps discovery but does not guarantee crawling
or indexing; internal linking and evidence remain necessary.

Implementation snapshot, 2026-07-27:

- 28 canonical URLs are in the public sitemap;
- the recorded Search Console snapshot has 23 indexed URLs and five current
  routes discovered but not yet indexed;
- the content-gated Google and Bing sitemap workflow is healthy and must not
  submit when indexable content has not changed;
- this change adds the first two roadmap articles, ready for publication, and
  a reusable direct-answer, takeaways, audience, and last-reviewed article
  contract; and
- service and primary case-study metadata now draw their target phrase from the
  typed content source of truth.

The numbers above are dated operating evidence, not a permanent claim about
Google's current dashboard. Recheck authenticated provider state before making
an external reporting or ranking claim.

## Article quality and information contract

Every published article must include:

1. a specific H1 and 40-60 word direct answer;
2. three to five key takeaways;
3. an explicit audience;
4. descriptive H2 sections for the problem, architecture or workflow,
   decision table, implementation example, failure modes, operating/security
   considerations, validation checklist, and when-not-to-use boundary where
   relevant;
5. a related service, a relevant case study, two supporting articles where
   relevant, and a Hire call to action;
6. visible author information, source links for changing factual claims, and a
   truthful last-reviewed date;
7. FAQ content only where it answers a real question and is visible in the
   article; and
8. explicit labels for concepts, experiments, in-development work, active R&D,
   and unverified external acceptance.

The shared article template now renders the direct answer, takeaways, audience,
author, and last-reviewed date. Content validation rejects missing required
frontmatter, summaries outside 40-60 words, or fewer than three or more than
five takeaways.

## Internal linking model

Each article should link naturally to:

- one relevant service page;
- one relevant case study or experiment;
- two supporting articles when available; and
- the appropriate Hire anchor.

Each service page should expose a primary case study, two to four supporting
articles, a real FAQ block, a consultation path, and a project-brief path.
Each case study should expose current maturity, related service, architecture
or reliability articles, public evidence, and the distinction between what
exists, what remains, and the next evidence gate.

Use descriptive anchors such as SaaS product engineering service, review-gated
AI commerce architecture, or retry-safe Stripe webhook design. Do not repeat
an exact-match anchor unnaturally or use ambiguous labels such as click here.

## Authority and distribution

After a genuinely useful article is published:

- create one LinkedIn summary, one architecture or decision post, and one
  lesson or failure-mode post;
- add a relevant link to a public repository README or profile only where it
  helps that repository's reader;
- write a condensed, canonical-linking version for a relevant community only
  when it adds a new audience and does not create a duplicate-content farm; and
- contribute useful technical answers and open-source documentation rather than
  promotional comments.

Each quarter, review public GitHub and LinkedIn entity consistency, pursue one
high-quality open-source documentation contribution, and seek a relevant
community, guest article, or podcast opportunity only when the audience fits
the services. Ask for a testimonial only after a real result and with explicit
publication permission.

## 28, 56, and 90-day execution checkpoints

### Days 1-28

- Recheck production positioning, title, description, canonical, and structured
  data consistency.
- Deploy the first two articles and connect them to the SaaS service and
  marketplace case study.
- Review the four core case studies for summary, current maturity, and internal
  links.
- Export the Search Console query and indexing baseline.
- Verify historical redirects for /projects, /lab, and /resume after the
  relevant deployment.

### Days 29-56

- Publish the next two substantial articles.
- Ensure every service page has at least two relevant supporting articles.
- Improve the marketplace case-study evidence only when new verified evidence
  exists.
- Align GitHub README and LinkedIn entity language with the portfolio.
- Review indexed pages with high impressions and low CTR for title and
  description clarity.

### Days 57-90

- Complete six new or materially refreshed articles across the period.
- Review query overlap and avoid creating another primary page for the same
  intent.
- Expand pages earning positions roughly 8-30 only when reader value and
  evidence support it.
- Measure service-to-Hire journeys, consultation and project-brief activity,
  and case-study-assisted enquiries without placing personal data in analytics.

## Monthly dashboard

Track the following as a product and business dashboard, not as vanity SEO:

- indexed URLs, discovered-but-not-indexed URLs, crawl errors, and structured
  data alerts;
- non-branded impressions, average position, Top 3, Top 10, and Top 20 terms
  by query cluster;
- CTR by landing page and high-impression query;
- service page visits and service-to-Hire click rate;
- consultation bookings, project-brief submissions, qualified leads, and
  article-assisted enquiries;
- public-work and GitHub outbound clicks;
- linking domains, Core Web Vitals, Bing AI Performance citations, and content
  update age.

The business question is always more important than raw traffic: which query,
case study, or article brought a qualified founder, product team, or research
conversation?

## Operational rules

- Changes to content/site.ts and published content/blog files are the only
  indexable-content triggers for search submission.
- The scheduled workflow may submit the sitemap only after an indexable content
  change and no more than once in a rolling 24 hours.
- Use the Search Console sitemap submission flow, not Google's restricted
  Indexing API for ordinary portfolio pages.
- Do not publish thin city, country, technology, or daily AI-generated pages.
- Do not invent client outcomes, production status, rankings, testimonials,
  backlinks, or field validation.
