# Client Acquisition Playbook

## Funnel

```text
Targeted outreach / GitHub / LinkedIn / search / referral
                         ↓
             service, case study, or article
                         ↓
          public proof + process + fit criteria
                         ↓
         `/hire#project-brief` (email fallback)
                         ↓
           discovery -> paid scope -> delivery
```

The site now supports the funnel. Consistent distribution and follow-up are still
owner-operated work; the portfolio cannot create qualified demand by itself.

Lead with `End-to-End Product Engineer for Software, AI & Connected/IoT
Products`. The sole on-site conversion path is `/hire#project-brief`; direct
email is a resilience fallback, while GitHub, LinkedIn, services, work, Journal,
and articles supply discovery and evidence rather than competing forms.

## Link selection

Send the narrowest useful page instead of always sending the homepage:

- `/hire#project-brief` for a referral, general product enquiry, or broad
  founder fit;
- `/services/ai-commerce-platform-engineering` for a commerce operator actively
  evaluating a build or modernization partner;
- `/work/ai-dropshipping-commerce-platform` when the prospect needs technical
  evidence before discussing scope;
- either AI dropshipping article when outreach begins with a specific
  architecture, review, pricing, campaign, or automation risk; and
- another service page when the observed problem is clearly software,
  AI-enabled, backend/realtime, or connected/IoT product work; every conversion
  CTA on those pages still ends at `/hire#project-brief`.

## Ideal client profile

Prioritize:

- a founder with a validated problem and authority to make product decisions;
- a small product team needing ownership of a defined engineering outcome;
- a company adding a practical AI workflow to an existing product;
- a team with backend, API, realtime, or integration delivery risk; or
- a research team needing a software-led connected prototype.

The connected/IoT boundary is discovery, architecture, software, integration,
and validated prototyping owned by Ariful, with specialist partners used for PCB
design, certification, production engineering, or manufacturing. Qualify or
refer leads that require unsupported in-house physical production capability.

Deprioritize vague “build the next X” requests with no user, budget, decision
owner, or willingness to narrow scope.

## Lead qualification

Capture in the project brief or first reply:

- product lane: Software, AI-enabled, Connected/IoT, or Cross-domain/Not sure;
- current lifecycle stage: Discovery, Scope/Architecture,
  Prototype/Validation, Build/Integration, Verify/Launch, Improve/Extend, or
  Not sure;
- target user and painful workflow;
- desired business or research outcome;
- what exists now;
- decision-maker and stakeholders;
- expected timeline and why it matters;
- budget range;
- security, data, compliance, hardware, or deployment constraints; and
- the decision the client wants the engagement to enable.

Classify each lead as:

1. **Fit now:** problem, authority, budget, and timing are sufficiently clear.
2. **Nurture:** credible fit but timing or budget is not ready.
3. **Refer:** legitimate work outside the service boundary.
4. **Decline:** risk, ethics, communication, or commercial fit is poor.

## Weekly operating rhythm

### Monday — target list

Select 10–15 companies or founders with an observable product signal: recent
launch, hiring need, public technical problem, funded roadmap, active repository,
or relevant research program. Record source, reason for fit, contact, next step,
and follow-up date in a simple CRM.

### Tuesday to Thursday — useful outreach

Send 3–5 researched messages per day. Reference a real product detail and one
specific risk or opportunity. Link to the closest case study or article, not the
homepage by default.

### Friday — follow-up and evidence

Follow up on previous messages, publish one technical insight or project update,
and record replies, calls, objections, and lead quality. Improve site copy using
repeated real objections rather than generic conversion advice.

## Outreach templates

### Founder or product lead

> Hi [Name] — I looked at [specific product/workflow]. The [specific observation]
> suggests [concrete risk or opportunity]. I provide end-to-end product
> engineering for software, AI-enabled, and connected products, and I
> wrote/built [relevant link] around a similar delivery decision. If [outcome]
> is on your roadmap, I can outline a small first scope and the main technical
> risks. Is that useful?

### Applied AI opportunity

> Hi [Name] — your [support/knowledge/operations] workflow looks like a possible
> fit for retrieval plus human-reviewed AI, but only if [specific data/workflow
> condition] is available. I use evaluation and approval boundaries rather than
> silently auto-applying model output. If you are exploring this, I can send a
> one-page architecture for the smallest reliable test.

### AI commerce or dropshipping platform

> Hi [Name] — I looked at [store/platform/workflow]. Your [supplier ingestion,
> localization, pricing, creative, campaign, checkout, or analytics step] appears
> to depend on [specific tool or manual handoff]. I built an AI commerce platform
> foundation around this exact operating boundary, with editable AI output and
> approval before consequential actions. Here is the closest [case study or
> article link]. If this is on your roadmap, I can outline the smallest vertical
> slice and the integration risks worth testing first. Would that be useful?

### Follow-up

> Following up once because [specific reason] still looks relevant. The smallest
> useful first step would be [audit/prototype/scope], not a full rebuild. If the
> timing is wrong, no response is needed.

Do not automate unsolicited high-volume messages. Relevance, evidence, and a
clear opt-out protect reputation and produce better conversations.

## Discovery call structure

Use 30 minutes:

1. 5 minutes: context, user, and desired change;
2. 10 minutes: current workflow, system, and evidence;
3. 5 minutes: constraints, stakeholders, budget, and timing;
4. 5 minutes: risks and possible first engagement; and
5. 5 minutes: decision, owner, and next action.

Do not design the complete solution for free. The call should establish fit and
whether a paid discovery, audit, prototype, or implementation scope is next.

## Engagement entry points

- **Architecture/AI readiness review:** bounded analysis with findings and a
  prioritized plan.
- **Prototype sprint:** tests one high-risk product, AI, or device assumption.
- **Defined product slice:** delivers one complete workflow into production.
- **Ongoing engineering partnership:** reserved for products with clear ownership,
  cadence, and backlog authority.

Price only after enough scope is known. Do not publish invented starting prices
until Ariful chooses a commercial model and validates it against actual projects.

Map each entry point to the six-stage lifecycle: Discover → Scope & Architecture
→ Design & Prototype → Build & Integrate → Verify & Launch → Handover & Iterate.
The engagement may start or stop at a defined stage, but handoff and the next
decision must remain explicit.

## CRM fields

Company, contact, role, source, product URL, fit reason, product lane, lifecycle
stage, service, observed signal, message date, reply, next follow-up, budget,
timeline, objection, outcome, and relevant portfolio link.

For AI commerce leads, also record the current supplier source, target markets,
storefront stack, AI workflows already in use, advertising channels, and the
single operational handoff causing the most cost or risk.

## Metrics

Track weekly:

- researched messages sent;
- positive reply rate;
- discovery calls;
- qualified opportunities;
- proposals or paid scopes;
- close rate;
- average lead time; and
- source and content that influenced the opportunity.

Review message quality before increasing volume.
